Variable-speed drives (VSDs), including variable-frequency drives (VFDs), allow the operating speed of suitable motor-driven equipment to be adjusted according to the required load. They are commonly considered for pumps, fans, compressors, and other applications where flow, pressure, or output requirements vary over time.

A VSD can reduce the need to control a fixed-speed motor by mechanical throttling or other less efficient methods. The potential benefit depends strongly on the equipment type, load profile, operating hours, control requirements, and existing system design.

Facilities should assess the complete motor-driven system before installing a VSD. The assessment should consider the motor, driven equipment, control method, minimum and maximum operating conditions, electrical compatibility, and maintenance requirements.

VSD installation is therefore a site-specific engineering intervention. It should be considered where variable demand exists and where speed control can provide an appropriate method of matching equipment output to actual requirements.

Source basis: U.S. Department of Energy guidance on variable-speed drives, pumps, fans, and motor systems.
