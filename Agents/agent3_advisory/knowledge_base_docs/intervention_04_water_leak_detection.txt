Unexpected increases in water consumption can indicate leaks, equipment problems, changes in operating conditions, or other forms of abnormal water use. Water consumption should be monitored against historical usage and operational activity rather than relying on a single universal percentage threshold. Facilities can establish a baseline by recording water-meter readings over time and comparing consumption with production levels, occupancy, or operating hours. An unexplained increase in consumption should trigger an investigation of common leak locations such as water-supply pipes, valves, tanks, cooling systems, toilets, and other water-using equipment. Leak detection should begin with meter and usage checks and can then progress to physical inspection or specialist leak-detection services when necessary. A water-use anomaly alone does not prove that a leak exists; operational changes and equipment use should also be considered.
